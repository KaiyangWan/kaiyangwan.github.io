Research

1. Designing systems around model limits

I study how to organize complex NLP tasks across model calls, and the reasoning limits that motivate these designs.

  • Findings of ACL 2025

    A Cognitive Writing Perspective for Constrained Long-Form Text Generation

    Kaiyang Wan, Honglin Mu, Rui Hao, Haoran Luo, Tianle Gu, Xiuying Chen

    CogWriter. A training-free system for planning, writing, and reviewing constrained long-form text. On LongGenBench, CogWriter with Qwen2.5-14B generates documents exceeding 10,000 words and outperforms GPT-4o on complex instruction completion.

  • ICLR 2026

    A Fano-Style Accuracy Upper Bound for LLM Single-Pass Reasoning in Multi-Hop QA

    Kaiyang Wan, Lang Gao, Honglin Mu, Preslav Nakov, Yuxia Wang, Xiuying Chen

    Fano bound / InfoQA. An information-theoretic bound on single-pass reasoning, which guides a task decomposition that limits information load and makes dependencies explicit. Experiments on noisy multi-hop QA follow the predicted capacity trends.

2. Understanding and optimizing each model

Inside each model, I study where task information forms, how reliably one can read it out, and how training shapes its organization.

  • NeurIPS 2026

    How Fine-Tuning Objectives Shape Layer-Wise Information in LLM Hallucination Detection

    Kaiyang Wan, Forrest Sheng Bao, Amin Ahmad, Yuxia Wang

    InfoWindow. Shows how fine-tuning objectives shape hallucination-detection information across layers. Truncating about 31% of model depth matches or improves full-depth performance on Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B.

  • Preprint

    How Language Models Organize Task Information

    Kaiyang Wan, Yuxia Wang

    k*. Studies how sparsely one can read task information from model representations across 19 language models and 14 tasks, connecting these measurements to labeling requirements and diagnosis after continual learning or pruning.

Next: To connect these measurements to optimization dynamics, so that agents can learn useful representations efficiently and evolve them as tasks change.
